Eighty per cent of marketers say they feel pressure to adopt AI.  
Six per cent have actually embedded it into their workflows.

That finding, from Supermetrics’ 2026 Marketing Data Report of 435 marketers across the US, UK, Germany, Australia and Singapore ([supermetrics.com](https://supermetrics.com/blog/marketing-data-report-2026)), is the most honest number in B2B marketing right now. We are calling the space between those two figures the Marketing Say-Do Gap, and it is where most marketing budgets currently go to die.

### **The gap is wider than anyone admits**

The pattern repeats across every serious dataset. Salesforce’s 2026 State of Marketing report, built on 4,450 marketing decision-makers ([salesforce.com](https://www.salesforce.com/news/stories/state-of-marketing-2026/)), found that 83% of marketers say customers now expect two-way conversations. In the same survey, 84% admitted they are still running generic, one-way campaigns. The CMO Survey from Duke Fuqua, Deloitte and the AMA puts a precise figure on execution depth: generative AI currently touches roughly 15% of all marketing activities ([CMO Survey summary](https://www.omnibound.ai/blog/marketing-ai-adoption-statistics)). The other 85% runs exactly as it did in 2022.

So what: stop asking your team whether they use AI. Ask which specific workflows would break tomorrow if the AI layer disappeared. If the answer is “none”, you have adoption theatre, and you are funding it.

### **Why the gap exists**

Supermetrics’ data points to a structural cause rather than a tooling one. Over half of marketing teams (52%) do not own their data strategy, and 40% cannot prove ROI across channels ([supermetrics.com](https://supermetrics.com/blog/marketing-data-report-2026)). AI cannot operate on fragmented, unowned data, so it stays in the sandbox: a headline generator here, a summarisation tool there. Impressive in a team meeting. Invisible in the P&L.

The uncomfortable read is that the Say-Do Gap is a leadership gap. Teams claim adoption because the pressure to claim it is real, then quietly run the old playbook because the foundations for anything else were never built.

So what: before your next AI line item is approved, require a named owner for marketing data, a defined use case, and a measurement method. Tools without those three are decoration.

### **What the 6% do differently**

The minority who have crossed the gap share three habits. They picked one workflow and automated it end to end rather than sprinkling AI across twenty tasks. They put marketing in charge of its own data rather than borrowing it from IT. And they treat ROI as a directional signal triangulated across methods (testing, ROI analysis, mix modelling) rather than demanding a single perfect number before acting.

None of this is glamorous. That is rather the point. The winners are doing boring integration work while everyone else is doing exciting announcements.

### **The line to remember**

Adoption is a claim. Integration is a receipt.

B2B marketing leaders heading into 2027 planning season should audit themselves against one question: if a customer traced your marketing output back through your operation, would they find AI doing structural work, or would they find a press release?

So what: close your own Say-Do Gap before your competitors close theirs. The 6% is not a fixed club. It is simply the group that started the unglamorous work eighteen months earlier than everyone else, and the compounding has already begun.
