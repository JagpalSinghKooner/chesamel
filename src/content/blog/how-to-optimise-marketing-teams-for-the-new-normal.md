Marketing has changed. Brands once dictated what was "cool” (think Apple with the iPhone), today, individuals shape trends. This shift places a greater responsibility on marketing teams to understand and connect with their audiences. And as debates around in-office, hybrid, or fully remote work continue, the focus on what makes a team truly effective has often been overshadowed. 

Let’s explore how to optimise marketing teams for this new normal, ensuring they remain agile, creative, and purpose-driven in a shifted industry.

## Embrace Hybrid Work for Creative Collaboration

Hybrid work models have become the new norm, offering a balance that fosters both creativity and collaboration. When done right, this blend of in-person teamwork and remote flexibility allows teams to benefit from the best of both worlds.

Companies have found success by implementing meeting-free half days. In some cases, this approach has increased focus time by more than 400%, highlighting the value of giving team members uninterrupted periods to engage in deep, creative work. The flexibility of hybrid work environments also encourages a broader exchange of ideas.

## Leverage AI and Tech without Replacing Creativity

AI and automation have revolutionised the way marketing teams operate, taking over repetitive tasks and providing valuable data insights. However, the key to success lies in using these tools to complement rather than replace human creativity.

Marketing teams must be trained to use AI tools effectively, ensuring that they enhance, rather than stifle, creative thinking. For instance, AI can provide insights that help marketers tailor content to individual preferences, but it’s the human element that crafts a compelling narrative around those insights. 

Balancing technology with creativity ensures that marketing strategies remain both data-driven and emotionally resonant.

## Prioritise a Purpose-Driven Mindset

Consumers are increasingly seeking authenticity, which is why a purpose-driven marketing team has never been more important. This means marketers must connect their work to a broader mission beyond just selling products.

Encouraging your team to align with the company’s overall goals and stay updated on industry trends can reignite their passion and drive innovation. For instance, marketers who understand the social or environmental impact of their products are better equipped to create campaigns that resonate deeply with audiences, ultimately building stronger brand loyalty.

## Rethink Meetings to Boost Productivity

Meeting overload is a common issue in modern workplaces, often leading to reduced productivity and creativity. [Microsoft Teams](https://globalnews.ca/news/9009563/online-meeting-times-new-work-models-needed/) data shows a 252% increase in meeting time since February 2020, a trend that has strained marketing teams and stifled their output.

To address this, companies should consider strategies like implementing meeting-free days or redefining the purpose of meetings. By focusing on creativity and brainstorming rather than routine updates, teams can reclaim valuable time for deep, uninterrupted work. 

This approach not only boosts productivity but also enhances the overall quality of creative output.

## Maximise the Value of Your Resources

People bring more to their roles than just their job titles. Marketing leaders should explore the unique insights and skills their teams offer. Especially when faced with tightening budgets from above. 

Flexibility and creativity are crucial in maximising team resources. Fostering an agile marketing approach that encourages experimentation and is open to strategy pivots can better prepare you for the unexpected. This adaptability ensures teams can effectively respond to challenges and seize opportunities, even with limited resources.
