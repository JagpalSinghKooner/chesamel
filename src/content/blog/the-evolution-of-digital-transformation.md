## What is digital transformation?

While digitalisation is the use of digital data to streamline processes and digitisation is the first shift from analogue to digital, a step that the majority of enterprises have now made, digital transformation can be seen as the total technological evolution of an organisation's operations.

The process of integrating digital technology into every aspect of an organisation, from internal business processes to customer service and value delivery, is known as "digital transformation." A company's digital transformation involves a cultural shift and overarching changes to its digital processes and systems. This comprehensive transition affects both customers and employees. The phrase digital transformation was [coined relatively recently](https://www.xperience-group.com/news-item/short-history-of-digital-transformation-the-worlds-longest-revolution/) but is now frequently cited as a [necessary step](https://www.forbes.com/sites/forbesbusinesscouncil/2021/09/23/why-the-era-of-digital-transformation-is-important-for-companies-of-all-sizes/?sh=6c8fe67c7a12) for all enterprises. Despite being a crucial transition for a company's survival, many digital transformation efforts fail because people misunderstand what it means and what it entails.

## The digital transformation evolution

Over the past few decades, as companies’ digital aspirations grew with the introduction of automated operations, computerised procedures and connectivity, technology departments quickly needed to manage these new platforms. This prompted businesses to exploit digital data for their own distinctive organisational activities and experiences.

With this new capacity for collaboration and data leveraging, [companies incorporated new digital platforms and approaches](https://capacity.com/digital-transformation/history-of-digital-transformation/), hoping to develop more flexibility while remaining competitive.

The theme has undergone impressive development over the past ten years. The stock prices of many technology companies reached new highs at the start of the global COVID-19 pandemic, as businesses sought to move a large portion of their operations online and [integrate cutting-edge technologies](https://www.mckinsey.com/industries/retail/our-insights/the-tech-transformation-imperative-in-retail) into every facet of their operations. This marked a new chapter in the evolution of digital transformation, as companies rapidly integrated cloud platforms, collaboration tools, and other digital technologies at scale.

Spending on digital transformational initiatives [significantly increased in 2020 and 2021](https://financesonline.com/digital-transformation-statistics/) as a result of the shift of workers to remote work, continued supply-chain disruptions, and the massive increase in online sales. Spending on these projects grew during the pandemic, sparking the next stage of digital transformation, despite overall cost savings in many operational areas.

To give you an example of the scale of this continual evolution, [41%](https://www.zendesk.co.uk/blog/zendesk-sales-trends-report-2021/) of sales leaders say their customers now want to communicate digitally more often than prior to the pandemic.

Due to these changing technologies and customer behaviours, digital transformation has become essential for all organisations to maintain their competitiveness. In fact, going forward, research suggests that by 2025, [70%](https://www.equinix.co.uk/resources/analyst-reports/gartner-digital-infrastructure-platforms) of organisations will help drive digital transformation by implementing structured infrastructure automation to deliver flexibility and efficiency, up from 20% in 2021.

## What makes a successful digital transformation in 2023?

According to research, fewer than [30%](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/people-and-organizational-performance/our-insights/unlocking-success-in-digital-transformations) of digital transformation initiatives succeed on average, which is a persistently low success rate.

A [McKinsey Global Survey](https://www.mckinsey.com/) conducted in 2018 sought to identify the factors that contribute to the success of digital transformations. A wide range of geographies, sectors, firm sizes, specialities, and tenures were represented in the respondents, who were questioned about any digital transformation initiatives they had worked on, either at their current or prior places of employment. 21 best practices were found among individuals who acknowledged success in the digital transitions they had participated in. These qualities can be divided into five groups:  

- Leadership
    
- Competence
    
- Development
    
- Worker empowerment
    
- Tool improvement
    

 

However, according to [54%](https://blog.kinly.com/collaboration-tech-enables-digital-transformation) of business owners, integrating all of these innovative initiatives and solutions into regular company operations is the biggest obstacle to achieving their digital transformation goals.

## The Evolution of Digital Transformation - FAQs

### What does digital transformation mean in business?

Digital transformation is the integration of digital technologies into all areas of a business, changing how it operates and delivers value to customers. It involves not only tools and platforms, but also cultural and organisational change.

### What technologies are shaping the future of digital transformation?

[Artificial intelligence, machine learning](/services/ai-machine-learning/), blockchain, and the Internet of Things (IoT) are some of the leading technologies set to shape the future of digital transformation in the coming years.

### How can businesses measure the ROI of digital transformation?

ROI can be tracked through improved efficiency, increased revenue, reduced costs, enhanced customer satisfaction, and higher employee productivity.

### Is digital transformation only for large enterprises?

No. Small and medium-sized businesses often benefit even more because digital solutions can help them compete with larger players and scale efficiently.
