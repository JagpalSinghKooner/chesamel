### Lessons from Chesamel’s first Resilience Roundtable

  
Confidence in Qatar’s future was a clear theme at Chesamel’s first Resilience Roundtable. The discussion also explored what businesses need to turn that confidence into progress: practical plans, capable people and the willingness to adapt.

Held on 9 November 2026 at Pullman Doha West Bay, The Resilience Roundtable: Economic Growth in 2027 & Beyond brought together leaders from across sectors. John Cofie, Founder and CEO of Chesamel Group, led the conversation, joined by Jason Zimmermann of Bloomberg.

Guests shared different experiences of growth in Qatar and the wider GCC. Alongside optimism about the market, they raised questions about investment priorities, digital change, contractor capability and support for entrepreneurs. These are some of the themes that emerged, and the practical questions they leave for businesses planning ahead.

#### **Confidence creates a foundation for growth**

Participants spoke positively about Qatar’s direction, stability and support for business. Several connected that confidence to national leadership, long-term strategy, local knowledge and the development of capability across sectors.

“Qatar serves as a platform for global expansion. The country is positioned for long-term stability and safety, which gives businesses confidence. World-class infrastructure, resources, and capital access are a strong foundation for businesses to grow.” **— Xiomara Henriquez, Executive Director, American Chamber of Commerce Qatar**

That confidence sat alongside an open discussion of operational challenges. Finding the right delivery partners can still be difficult. Business decisions may need to be reassessed as supply chains change. Organisations also need people who can put their plans into practice.

A useful takeaway is to look at the foundations within each business as closely as the opportunity around it. Which skills, relationships and processes support the next stage of growth? Where is the business relying on an assumption that needs to be tested?

#### **Digital transformation needs a practical plan**

Technology was a recurring part of the discussion, including its potential to make work easier and help businesses adapt. John’s question about implemented digital transformation policies brought the conversation back to execution.

The question matters because an ambition needs an agreed route into everyday work. Teams need to understand what will change, why it matters and how they can contribute.

For a business working through digital transformation, that means being clear about the problem to solve, who is responsible and how progress will be measured. A policy, a strategy and a working process serve different purposes; each needs to connect to what people actually do.

The next step may be a focused improvement to one process rather than a broad transformation programme. Testing it with the people who use it can help reveal where training, better information or changes to responsibilities are needed.

This connects with Chesamel’s approach to [digital transformation](/services/digital-transformation/), which helps organisations identify gaps, set priorities and develop a practical path to delivery.

#### **People need support as technology changes**

The human element came through clearly. Participants discussed the importance of adapting, learning and working together as the pace of digital change increases.

“Qatar’s resilience is rooted in the strength, adaptability and ambition of its people. By investing in our talent and developing future capabilities, we build on that resilience, turning challenges into opportunities for growth and emerging stronger with every experience.” **— Fadia Fakih, Head of Talent & Resourcing, Commercial Bank**

Entrepreneurship brought this into focus. Having an idea and a willingness to invest does not always make the first decisions easy. Founders may need guidance on where to begin, how to prioritise spending and which capabilities to build.

Mentoring and practical support were raised as ways to help. For established organisations, the same question applies to teams: do people have the skills, confidence and clarity to deliver the plan?

Our takeaway is that workforce planning belongs within the growth conversation from the start. Understanding the roles and capabilities needed can help businesses make better decisions about technology, delivery and how they reach customers.

#### **Fast learning and patient investment can work together**

John’s question about the next 90 days prompted a discussion about the time it can take for investment to produce a return. It also brought out the need to learn quickly and respond to change.

Those time horizons need to be considered together. A longer-term investment still needs useful checkpoints. A fast decision still needs a clear reason and a way to assess what happens next.

“We see strong, sustained momentum across key growth sectors like energy, technology, healthcare, and defence — backed by a level of stability that benefits both businesses and the people living and working here.” **— Bara' Sami, Group General Manager, Mekdam Holding Group**

For leaders planning ahead, the practical question is what progress should look like before the financial outcome is clear. That might mean testing an assumption, developing a capability or finding out whether a proposed approach meets a real customer need.

#### **Better support starts with better questions**

One contribution asked what corporate businesses need from banks so that support can be more relevant. Elsewhere, the conversation raised contractor availability and the effects of business decisions on supply chains.

These points underline the value of bringing different perspectives together. A provider’s understanding of the problem may differ from the organisation experiencing it. Listening carefully can help both sides identify where support would be useful.

For Chesamel, the discussion reinforces the importance of understanding a business’s priorities before recommending a solution. Digital capability, workforce needs and marketing decisions often affect one another. Considering them together can make the next step clearer.
